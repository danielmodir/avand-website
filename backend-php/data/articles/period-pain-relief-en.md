Period pain (dysmenorrhoea) is very common: according to the WHO, more than two thirds of people who menstruate experience it. For mild to moderate pain there are simple steps that help.

## Heat

A **hot water bottle or heat pad** on the tummy, or a warm bath, is one of the self-care steps the NHS suggests. In systematic reviews, heat relieved pain about as well as, or better than, common painkillers, although the certainty of this evidence is still low.

## Massage and gentle movement

- **Gentle massage** of the tummy and lower back can ease discomfort.
- **Light activity** such as walking, yoga, swimming or cycling is also in the NHS advice.

## Regular exercise

In a recent systematic review of 29 trials, regular exercise reduced period pain, with strength training working best. The most effective programmes lasted at least 8 weeks, with more than 3 sessions a week of over 30 minutes each. Most participants were young women with regular cycles.

## Painkillers

Paracetamol and ibuprofen are over-the-counter options the NHS mentions. **Your doctor or pharmacist should decide which one and how much is right for you**; Toojibee never suggests a medicine or a dose.

## Supplements

A Cochrane review found no high-quality evidence that any supplement works for period pain; ginger has limited, low-quality evidence of benefit. Ask a doctor or pharmacist before taking any supplement.

## When to see a doctor

- the pain **stops you doing everyday things**, or gets worse month after month;
- it comes with **pain during sex, or when going to the toilet**;
- your bleeding is heavy (for example, changing pads every 1 to 2 hours).

**If the pain is severe or keeps getting worse despite painkillers, get medical help urgently.**
